I've taken three big professional bets in the last decade: leaving a stable engineering role to join sales engineering, leaving a job I loved to start Shake, and a year ago, joining LaunchDarkly's pre-IPO push into AI. Each time, friends asked the same question: "How do you know it's the right move?"

The honest answer is: I don't. But I've gotten better at noticing what kind of risk I'm actually taking.

## The four kinds of risk I keep mixing up

Most decisions aren't one risk — they're four, and we tend to overweight the one that's easiest to picture and underweight the rest.

**Financial risk** is the loudest. It's the one your spouse asks about, the one your spreadsheet models, the one that wakes you at 4 AM. It's also, in my experience, the most recoverable. I took on €30K of personal debt closing Shake. It was awful. It was also gone three years later.

**Career risk** is sneakier. The choice that "looks weird on your CV" is often the one that compounds best — but only if you survive the next two years of explaining it in interviews. The market doesn't reward eclectic résumés the way LinkedIn comments suggest it does.

**Identity risk** is the one nobody talks about. Who do you become if this doesn't work? I spent six months after Shake unable to introduce myself at a party. "I used to run a startup" is a sentence I had to learn to say out loud.

**Relationship risk** is the one that should keep you up at night. Every founder I respect has a story about the partner, the friend, the parent who stuck with them through the bad years. The bet you're really making is on those people's patience.

## The framework I actually use

Before any big bet, I write three things on an index card:

1. The version where this works.
2. The version where this fails on a 24-month horizon.
3. What I'd do on day one of version 2.

If version 2's day one is "panic", the bet is too big. If it's "send three emails and book a flight to Munich", the bet is probably right.

Self-awareness is the multiplier on everything else. You don't need to be fearless. You just need to know exactly which fear is talking.
