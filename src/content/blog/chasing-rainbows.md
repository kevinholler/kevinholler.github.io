A little over 10 years have passed since I graduated college in Ireland. In that time I started three companies (two failed), worked with five startups at varying stages, and somehow ended up writing software, selling it, and supporting it — sometimes in the same week.

Looking back, a few things have become very clear. My career path has been everything but linear. The three years of my first startup taught me more about myself, people, and business than any other period of my life. And despite the pain of shutting down a business, I've been crazy enough to attempt it again.

## Why I started writing

When my first company failed, I wrote a blog post announcing we were shutting down — detailing some of the factors that contributed. In Ireland at the time, failure was rarely spoken about openly. Realising I had already lost everything, I figured I had nothing left to lose.

I published the post, shut my laptop, and went for a walk.

What followed twenty-four hours later shocked me: a stream of job offers, interview requests from media, founders emailing to share similar experiences. The thing I was most afraid to say out loud was the thing other people most needed to hear.

## What I'm committing to

The stories I share will lean on four pillars. **Vulnerability** — no hiding behind the glamorous curtain of Silicon Valley success theatre. **Honesty** — facts only, even the embarrassing ones. **Humility** — leaving ego and pride at the door. **Insightful and fun** — life is too short for self-important essays.

Let's see where this journey takes us.
